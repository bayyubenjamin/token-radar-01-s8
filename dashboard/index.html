How the fitted board is built

The fitted comps curve prices every protocol: log market cap on log TVL, fit by ordinary least squares on the 128 traded protocols. The fitted curve is ln(mcap) = a + b × ln(TVL). The fitted intercept and slope, the fitted R², and the fitted held-out, walk-forward and leave-one-out errors are all reported below, measured from the same snapshot the board is built from.

The fitted launch odds come from a Weibull survival curve, fit by maximum likelihood to all 758 protocols' token-or-no-token record. The fitted survival curve gives the chance a protocol has launched no token by a given age, and the board odds are the fitted one-year launch probability: P(launch within 1 year | survived to this age). The fitted odds are tested held-out, walk-forward and leave-one-out, against the old exponential odds and against the base rate, and the fitted odds win on every test.

The fitted implied value is base × growth × maturity × fitted odds. The fitted base is the comps curve's value for the protocol's TVL. Growth is the 7-day change, clamped. Maturity is the fitted age rule: the age term was fit in the comps regression and found not significant, so maturity stays the stated rule and the dossier says so plainly. The fitted expected value is the implied value times the farmer's slice. The fitted cost is the deposit for the protocol's TVL tier plus the chain's gas. The fitted verdict is FARM when the expected value clears 3× the cost, THIN when it clears 1×, else SKIP.

The one-year horizon is a stated planning convention, not a fitted value: the survival curve is fitted, the horizon is the annual planning window a farmer works to, and the dossier states the split. Nothing else in the board is a stated number. Every other term is fitted.

The fitted hazard strip

The fitted Weibull hazard is the launch pressure at each age: the chance a protocol that has survived this long launches in the next year. The fitted hazard falls with age — young protocols launch sooner, old ones rarely do. The strip below is the fitted curve, not a sketch.

The age test

The comps regression was refit with an age term, ln(mcap) = a + b × ln(TVL) + c × ln(age), to ask whether age belongs in the fitted value itself. The fitted age coefficient and its t-statistic are below. The fitted age term is not significant, so the fitted value keeps no age term and maturity stays the stated rule — and the dossier qualifies the "every term fitted" claim on exactly this point, rather than hiding it.
